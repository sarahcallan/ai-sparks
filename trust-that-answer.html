Start here

AI gives you a draft, not a final answer. This spark teaches you how to check that draft fast, so you can use it with confidence.

You’ll need an AI tool open. If you did “Anatomy of a Strong Prompt,” keep that output handy.

Sounds right. Isn’t.

“Documents are due Thursday. Late submissions get an automatic two-week extension.”

The prompt said Friday. It also said no extensions.

What to notice

Nothing about that sentence looks wrong. It’s clear, confident, and friendly. That’s exactly why it’s risky: AI mistakes rarely look like mistakes.

Why it happens

  • It predicts likely words, not facts.It writes what usually comes next, whether or not it’s true.
  • It fills gaps with plausible details.Missing information gets invented, not flagged.
  • It sounds sure even when it’s guessing.Confident tone isn’t a sign of accuracy.

The takeaway

You don’t need to understand how AI works to use it well. You just need to remember that it’s a fast writer, not a fact-checker. You’re the fact-checker.

Four quick checks

FactsAre names, dates, places, and policies true?
NumbersDo figures and math match your source?
GapsIs anything missing that you asked for?
FitIs the tone right? Is it fair to everyone it describes?

How long this takes

For a short email, about a minute. Read it once with each check in mind.

About “fair”

AI can repeat stereotypes or leave groups out without meaning to. Ask: would everyone this is about feel it describes them accurately and respectfully?

Find five mistakes in this AI draft.

Hi there,

Your visa documents are due this Thursday. Over 80% of your classmates have already submitted, so don’t be the one holding everyone up!

Upload your documents through the study abroad portal. If you miss the deadline, you’ll automatically receive a two-week extension.

See you abroad!

The prompt: due Friday, a third haven’t submitted, warm but direct, no extensions, include who to contact.

How to look

Use the four checks: Facts, Numbers, Gaps, and Fit. Compare the draft against what the prompt asked for.

Why the extension matters most

A wrong day is annoying. An invented policy creates a promise your office now has to deal with. Made-up rules, deadlines, and policies are the errors to hunt for first.

Ask the AI to check its own work.

  1. “What are you least sure about in this answer?”
  2. “List every fact I should verify.”
  3. “Compare this to my instructions. What did you miss?”

Helpful, not enough

Self-checks catch a lot, but the AI can miss its own mistakes. Use them to find where to look. Your four checks are still the final word.

Before you paste anything in, ask:

  1. Does it name a student, patient, or colleague?
  2. Is it from a confidential record?
  3. Would it be a problem if it reached the wrong person?

Any yes? Remove it, swap in placeholders like [Student name], or check what NU allows.

Quick practice: safe or not?

  • A blank email template: safe
  • Last term’s reminder with names removed: safe
  • A student’s grades: not safe
  • An advising note about a specific student: not safe

When in doubt

Leave it out. Student records, health information, and confidential data have rules. Check with your manager or the university’s AI guidance first.

Your check card

Before I paste: No names, no confidential records, nothing I’d regret sending to the wrong person.

Facts: Are names, dates, places, and policies true?

Numbers: Do figures match my source?

Gaps: Did it cover everything I asked for?

Fit: Is the tone right, and is it fair?

Ask it: “What are you least sure about? List every fact I should verify.”

Keep this
