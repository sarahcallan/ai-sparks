Start here

You’re not learning a tool yet. First, you’re finding the right problem. By the end, you’ll have one task from your own work and a plan for it.

Not every task is an AI task. If you decide a task isn’t right for AI, that counts as a win.

All you need is paper and a pen. Use the arrow keys or the Next button to move through. Each step has a timer if you want to keep yourself moving.

Before

Write the weekly reminder email from scratch.

AI drafts the message.

What to notice

Only one part of this task used AI. The rest was ordinary automation that already exists in tools you have. That’s normal, and it’s often where the easiest time savings hide.

1

Write down one task you do every week that you wish took less time.

For example:

  • Answering the same questions by email
  • Cleaning up a spreadsheet
  • Writing a recurring report

Keep it small

Pick something you do weekly that takes under two hours. If your first idea is big, like “fix our whole process,” ask yourself: what’s one piece of that I do every week?

Stuck? Try one of these

  • Answering the same questions from students or colleagues
  • Renaming or organizing files after a mail merge
  • Summarizing feedback from an event or survey
  • Drafting routine outreach emails

How to break it down

Walk through the task like you’re explaining it to a new colleague. After each step, ask yourself “Then what?” until you reach the end.

Working with someone? Have them ask “Then what?” for you. It’s easier to spot missing steps out loud.

3

For each step, ask:

  1. Do I do it the same way every time?
  2. Is it mostly writing, sorting, or summarizing?
  3. Could I spot a mistake quickly?

Three yeses? Put a star next to it.

  • Pull the student list ★
  • Write the message ★
  • Add names and CCs ★
  • Answer replies

How to answer

Go step by step and answer honestly. A quick yes or no is enough.

A step with no star isn’t a failure. It means that step needs your judgment.

Why “Answer replies” has no star

Every reply is different, and a wrong answer to a student is hard to catch. That step stays with the person who knows the student.

4

For each starred step, choose the help.

First: does it use student, health, or confidential information? Then use only NU-approved tools, or keep it.
Ask itPrompt an AI tool
Teach itGive AI your files and examples
Automate itSet rules once, no AI needed
Keep itStays with you

What each option means

  • Ask it: type a request into an AI tool, like “Draft a reminder email about the deadline.”
  • Teach it: give the AI your own examples or files first, so the output sounds like you.
  • Automate it: set up rules once, like mail merge or a spreadsheet formula. No AI needed.
  • Keep it: this step needs your judgment, or the information is too sensitive.

Not sure about the data?

When in doubt, keep it. Student records, health information, and confidential data have rules. Check with your manager or the university’s AI guidance before using AI with them.

A tip for later

If you find yourself typing the same prompt three times, that’s your sign to move from “Ask it” to “Teach it.” Save your examples so the AI starts from your voice every time.
